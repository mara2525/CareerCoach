## Question 1 - Where is AI delivering real value today, and where is it falling short?

**Real value**

1. Logistics and freight optimization - your work at Kontango is a direct example. ML-driven freight market intelligence cut through noise that was previously just analyst intuition.
2. Agentic automation of repetitive operational workflows - commodity trading and supply chain have endless manual, rules-based tasks (document review, scheduling updates, price monitoring alerts) where AI agents are producing real ROI right now.
3. Decision support for procurement and trading desks - predictive analytics surfacing signals that humans then act on, with the human staying in the loop.

**Where it falls short**

1. AI deployed on top of ungoverned, siloed data. Most ag enterprises have not solved the data quality problem underneath. The AI reflects whatever chaos lives in the source systems.
2. Autonomous forecasting in highly seasonal, weather-dependent contexts where training data is sparse or inconsistent.
3. Anywhere the expectation is that the AI tool replaces strategy. Tools don't replace judgment.

---

## Question 2 - What challenges in agriculture present genuine opportunities, and where is the hype outpacing reality?

**Genuine opportunity**

1. Supply chain and logistics visibility. Between Roger and Kontango, you saw firsthand how hard it is for large ag shippers to get a real-time picture of where product is and what it costs to move. AI can solve this.
2. Commodity price intelligence and risk exposure. Trading desks are still working with fragmented data. Predictive models on clean, governed data are a genuine accelerator.
3. Compliance documentation and audit trails. AI can reduce the manual burden of SOX, CCPA, and food safety regulatory reporting dramatically.

**Where it is more hype than practical**

1. Precision ag hardware AI - the sensors, drones, and field data products are exciting, but the underlying data pipeline problem is rarely solved first. Enterprises are buying the shiny tool before the data infrastructure exists to support it.
2. End-to-end autonomous decision-making in trading. High-stakes, low-frequency, consequence-heavy decisions still require human judgment and legal accountability.

## Question 3 - Where can AI realistically solve problems, and where do traditional approaches still win?

**AI wins**

1. Pattern recognition across large, complex datasets - freight rates, position exposure, market signals.
2. Automating the analyst layer - summarizing, flagging, drafting - so your people spend time on the 20% that requires judgment.
3. Pulling signal from unstructured data - contracts, emails, reports - that was previously unanalyzable at scale.

**Traditional approaches still win**

1. High-stakes, low-frequency decisions where the consequences of a wrong call are financial or legal - hedging positions, compliance sign-offs, major procurement commitments.
2. Relationship-driven sales and negotiation. Ag is still a relationship business at the top.
3. Anything requiring institutional knowledge that has never been captured in data. The experienced grain merchandiser's instincts are not in any training set.

## Question 4 - What is one AI capability people should be paying closer attention to right now?

**Agentic workflows.**

Not AI that answers questions - AI that takes actions. Agents that monitor markets and surface alerts, agents that draft and route compliance documents, agents that trigger logistics workflows when conditions are met. This is the shift from AI as a search tool to AI as an operational layer.

Your framing from your 90-Day AI Accelerator is directly relevant here - most clients arrive thinking "chatbot" and leave with a production agentic workflow running on their actual systems. The leaders who understand this distinction in the next 12 months are going to move significantly faster than those who are still evaluating generic AI tools.

---

## Question 5 - What is one thing agricultural leaders should do today to prepare for the future impact of AI?

**Get the data house in order before buying the AI tool.**

Every enterprise AI failure you have seen traces back to the same root cause - governed data consumption patterns were not in place before the AI was deployed. AI runs on data. If the data is siloed, dirty, or ungoverned, the AI reflects that.

Concretely, this means three things for ag leaders today - first, identify which data domains are actually decision-critical and own them properly. Second, stop letting AI tools query source systems directly and start building curated data products the AI can safely consume. Third, build an AI use case backlog before signing any contracts - know what problem you are solving and what data it requires.

The companies that do this work now will compress 12-month AI pilots into 90-day production cycles. The ones that skip it will spend 18 months cleaning up.
