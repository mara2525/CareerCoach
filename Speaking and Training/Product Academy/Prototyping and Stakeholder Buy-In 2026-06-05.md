## 0-5 min - Opening + Quick Poll

Set the frame.

1. Poll 1 - "How often do you prototype?" (Never / Sometimes / Often)
2. Poll 2 - "What's your biggest stakeholder challenge?" (Can't 'see it' / No time / Too many opinions / Fear of being wrong)

Promise to the room - Today is about prototypes as a way to create shared understanding and decisions, not just artifacts.

## 5-12 min - The Case for Prototyping with Stakeholders

Link - https://scc-exec-dashboard-prod.azurewebsites.net/strategy-library/prototyping-demos/prototype-benefits

Stakeholder value lens - the five things stakeholders actually care about. For PMs, "stakeholders" means engineering, design, exec sponsors, and business partners - all at once.

1. Clarity - what are we building or changing? (replaces the PRD nobody reads)
2. Risk reduction - what could go wrong before we commit a sprint?
3. Speed - how fast can we learn without a full build?
4. Alignment - are engineering, design, and the business solving the same problem?
5. Confidence - should we invest or proceed to the next stage?

---

## 12-20 min - What a Prototype Is and Is Not

Link - https://scc-exec-dashboard-prod.azurewebsites.net/strategy-library/prototyping-demos/what-a-prototype-is

"Prototype" can mean many things - paper sketch, storyboard, clickable flow, service role-play, physical mock.

Script you can reuse - "Nothing about this is real, except what we're trying to learn."

Fidelity mapped to intent:

1. Explore options - low fidelity
2. Validate a flow - medium fidelity
3. De-risk feasibility or exec confidence - higher fidelity

## 20-33 min - Stories + Audience Discussion

Format - ask the discussion question first, let 2-3 people respond, then anchor the conversation with the story. The goal is recognition, not debate - keep each discussion to 2-3 minutes max.

### Discussion 1 - The Direction Approval Problem

Discussion question - "Has anyone had a stakeholder say 'that's not what I pictured' after something was already built? What would earlier visibility have changed?"

What you're listening for - war stories about late-stage pivots, rework, or stakeholder surprises.

Anchor story - artists get sketch approval before they paint. Stakeholders sign off on direction before final polish. The PM parallel - a wireframe review that surfaces the exec wanted a completely different entry point, before anyone spent a sprint building the wrong one. The cost of a prototype is always lower than the cost of a rebuild.

### Discussion 2 - The "Can't See It" Problem

Discussion question - "What's the hardest feature or experience you've tried to get stakeholder alignment on from a doc or a slide? What made it hard for them to engage?"

What you're listening for - examples of abstract concepts, new user flows, or anything that required spatial or experiential understanding.

Anchor story - stakeholders cannot approve a PRD the way they can approve something they experience. A requirements doc describes; a prototype demonstrates. Show the flow in context - a clickable walkthrough in a stakeholder meeting lands differently than a spec they read alone.

### Discussion 3 - Prototype the Riskiest Assumption

Discussion question - "When you think about something you're working on right now, what's the one assumption that, if wrong, would invalidate the whole feature?"

What you're listening for - scoping instincts. Most PMs will name something real.

Anchor story - prototype the latch, not the whole window. If you're building a new window design, the latch is the riskiest part - that's what you test first. The PM parallel - if the risk is "will users understand this new mental model?", prototype that screen, not the full checkout flow around it.

## 33-43 min - Modern Acceleration: AI Prototyping

The point isn't that AI is cool. The point is that PMs no longer have an excuse to wait for design or engineering capacity before getting early feedback. You can now:

1. Produce variants quickly - show two directions in the same meeting
2. Test narratives and flows - pressure-test your positioning before the roadmap review
3. Get feedback earlier - from users, from engineers, from execs, before anyone has committed to a direction

Key message - Speed increases your responsibility to show work sooner, not later. The "we didn't have time to prototype" era is over.

## 43-50 min - Stakeholder-Ready Prototype Checklist + Close

Link - https://scc-exec-dashboard-prod.azurewebsites.net/strategy-library/ai-assessments/stakeholder-readiness-checklist

Give the room a repeatable tool. PMs live in the decision-forcing business - this checklist maps directly to how they already think about stakeholder meetings and discovery reviews.

1. What decision do we need from stakeholders? (not "feedback" - a specific decision)
2. What are the 1-2 risks we're trying to reduce with this prototype?
3. What should they react to, and what should they ignore? (set this expectation out loud at the start)
4. What question are we asking at the end?
5. What's the next step if we get a "yes"? If we get a "no"?

Close with a final poll - "What will you prototype next week?" - plus a one-sentence recap.
