# SWARM Engineering: Business Model Analysis

**Date**: 2026-04-08  
**Based on**: Meetings with Yaz Abutaleb (Head of Customer Ops) and Joe Intrakamahang (CTO)

---

## Core Diagnosis

SWARM is running a **high-touch, bespoke consulting business** disguised as a SaaS product. They have not resolved whether they are a consulting firm or a product company, and this fundamental ambiguity is driving organizational stress.

---

## Evidence

### 1. Implementation Model Reveals Consulting Nature
1. 8-10 week implementation per customer (not product onboarding)
2. Custom data integration and schema mapping work on every engagement
3. One-time $15K setup fee + $10-15K monthly recurring
4. Setup fee exists because each customer is a custom project

### 2. Product Strategy Driven by Revenue Pressure
1. 23+ algorithms solving different problem sets
2. Sales team needs broad offering to hit targets
3. Board-level debate: focus on few algorithms or keep expanding?
4. Revenue pressure drives feature sprawl, not market need

### 3. CTO Owns Professional Services
1. Joe's role: "Technology + Product + Customer Success + Professional Services"
2. This is not a product company structure
3. Professional services ownership at CTO level indicates services-first business model

### 4. Reactive Delivery Dominates
1. Joe is "always reactive vs proactive"
2. No capacity for strategic product thinking
3. No quarter+ planning horizon
4. Constant scrambling to service current customers

### 5. Problem-to-Solution Gap Is Consulting Diagnosis Work
1. Hiring a Head of Product to "translate customer problems to algorithm selection"
2. Attempting to "automate problem-to-solution mapping"
3. This is consulting expertise, not a product feature
4. Cannot be scaled through automation

### 6. Only One Use Case Is Repeatable
1. Inventory allocation: started to mature, multiple customers, path to productization
2. Everything else (transportation, labor assignment, site location): custom per customer
3. Portfolio looks like consulting engagement mix, not product portfolio

### 7. Deal Characteristics Are Consulting, Not SaaS
1. Complex RFPs requiring $1M+ and 10 people from SWARM side
2. SaaS deals do not require this resource commitment
3. Transportation optimization "win" saving customer $500K+ is project delivery, not product adoption

### 8. Customers Asking for Explainability
1. Customers want platform to explain algorithm selections
2. Fundamentally a consulting delivery and training problem
3. Attempting to solve through platform features instead of through service delivery

---

## Why SWARM Doesn't Need a Head of Product

A Head of Product role assumes:

1. Clear product vision exists
2. Product-market fit is achieved and scaling
3. Engineers are building features, not customizing for clients
4. Good problems to prioritize among competing features

SWARM has none of these. Hiring a Head of Product is theater. A product leader would arrive, build a roadmap, and immediately realize: "Half this company is delivering services to sustain revenue. I cannot do product strategy in this environment."

The bottleneck is not product leadership. It is **business model clarity**.

---

## What SWARM Actually Needs

1. **Chief Services Officer** : Professionalize delivery, create playbooks, scale implementation efficiency, make 8-week onboarding repeatable

2. **VP of Sales with Discipline** : Align GTM to a specific motion; stop pushing engineering team into new use cases to chase revenue

3. **CEO/Board Decision** : Resolve the core question:
   a. Are you a consulting firm using technology to deliver? (Optimize for that.)
   b. Are you a product company with services as a ramp? (Stop treating services as core business model.)
   c. Are you transitioning from consulting to product? (When, and what gets cut?)

4. **VP of Operations or VP of Customer Success** : Manage delivery side, customer retention, logistics of running a professional services business

---

## Why This Matters for Joining

If you join SWARM as Head of Product:

1. You are doing consulting work inside an organization (same work as independent consultant, but with organizational overhead)
2. You are responsible for productizing something structurally tied to consulting delivery
3. You inherit constant tension between delivering for revenue and building repeatable solutions
4. You have limited autonomy and pricing power compared to independent consulting

**You should pass.** You are already solving this problem independently. The organization would benefit from someone like you, but you would not benefit from the organization.
